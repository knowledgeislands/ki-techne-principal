# Vision

## Purpose

Techne is the canonical engineering discipline for the Knowledge Islands ecosystem.

It turns the ecosystem's philosophy into repeatable engineering practice: architecture, operating models, governance, workflows, and implementation patterns.

Techne is a living knowledge base.

It records the reasoning behind engineering choices as well as the current state of the platform, so that practice can evolve without losing context.

## Vision

Enable engineering teams to build and operate AI-native systems that are capable, governable, observable, and adaptable.

The intended result is an engineering environment in which human intent, agent reasoning, deterministic operations, persistent execution, and governed knowledge have clear responsibilities and well-defined interfaces.

AI is treated as an engineering capability that must be designed, evaluated, operated, and reviewed with the same discipline applied to any other production concern.

## Relationship to Arcadia

Arcadia defines why Knowledge Islands exists and the principles that guide the wider ecosystem.

Techne defines how those principles are applied in engineering practice.

Techne therefore interprets, but does not replace, the philosophy established by Arcadia.

## Scope

Techne covers the engineering concerns needed to evolve the ecosystem coherently, including:

- platform and system architecture;
- AI execution and workload routing;
- engineering operating models and workflows;
- local, cloud, and remote engineering environments;
- tooling evaluation and technology lifecycle management;
- security, governance, and operational consequences; and
- implementation patterns for Knowledge Islands.

The knowledge base is implementation-neutral where possible.

Specific technologies are documented as current choices, evaluations, or examples; they are not treated as permanent architectural requirements unless an explicit decision records that commitment.

## Audiences

Techne is written for engineers who build and operate the platform, technical leaders who make or review architectural decisions, and contributors who need to understand how engineering knowledge is organised and evolved.

Each chapter should stand on its own while linking to the decisions, diagrams, evaluations, and roadmap items that provide its context.

## Evolution

Techne develops through small, reviewable increments.

Significant architectural changes should state their rationale, record alternatives and consequences in an Architecture Decision Record, update affected diagrams, and align the Technology Radar where relevant.

Where the architecture is incomplete or uncertain, Techne records the assumption or a clearly scoped TODO rather than presenting conjecture as settled design.
